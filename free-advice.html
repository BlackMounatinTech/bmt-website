FREE GAME / FOR CONTRACTORS

First, get a lead.

You need someone who wants the work you do.
Here’s where to find them—and how to get started.

01

Say what you do.

Pick a service and an area. Make it easy to know when to call you.

“We build fences in Courtenay and Comox.”

02

Look trustworthy.

Use real job photos, a clear website, and an up-to-date Google profile with honest reviews. People will look you up.

03

Pick one way to get leads.

Choose from the options below. Give it a proper test, keep track of inquiries, and build from there.

Where leads come from.

You don’t need to do all of these. Start with what fits your budget and your business.

01

People who know you

Customer referrals, repeat work, and old quotes.

Start here

Check in with five appropriate past customers or open quotes. Ask whether they still need the work or know someone who does.

02

Your online presence

Google and reviews, your website, organic social posts, Yelp, and local Facebook groups.

Start here

Post one real project with photos, your service area, and a clear way to contact you. Check your business details are current.

03

Paid advertising

Google search ads and Facebook or Instagram ads.

Start here

Promote one service in one area. Set a test budget you can afford and track suitable inquiries—not just clicks.

04

Local relationships

Other trades, builders, general contractors, property and strata/HOA managers, realtors, suppliers, and rental shops.

Start here

Introduce yourself to three businesses that serve the same customers. Show your work and explain which jobs you handle.

05

Being seen around town

Door hangers, neighbourhood postcards, jobsite signs, branded vehicles, billboards, radio, home shows, and local events.

Start here

Choose a small area or one placement to test. Keep your message readable: what you do, where you work, and how to reach you.

06

Community sponsorships

Local hockey teams, baseball teams, and community organizations.

Start here

Ask what a sponsorship includes and who it reaches. Support a good local fit; don’t assume a logo placement guarantees jobs.

Make it easy to trust you.

A referral, a postcard, or a team jersey can get someone interested. Your website, photos, and reviews help them decide whether to call. Keep building that proof as you finish more jobs.
