From August 19–31, 2026, we ran a Facebook Messenger campaign for our Vancouver Island construction business. Someone seeing the ad could click through to message the company about the work they needed. During those 13 days, the campaign recorded 20 messaging conversations from $189.54 CAD in ad spend—an average of $9.48 per conversation.

That gave the business a place to ask about the project, find out where it was, and discuss arranging an estimate. Starting that conversation is the role of the ad; quoting and winning the job comes next.

These are conversations started through the ad, not confirmed booked jobs.

The kind of work matters

You don’t need hundreds of enquiries.
You need the right projects.

Across the two years shown, Black Mountain Dirt Works sent 131 quote records totalling $1,662,724.46 CAD. Spread across 24 months for comparison, that’s about 5.5 quotes and $69,280 in quoted work per month. A relatively small number of quotes can still represent a significant amount of potential work.

We don’t win every quote. Our preference is to price fewer, larger projects and win a couple of substantial jobs, rather than fill the schedule with a high volume of smaller jobs. Bigger isn’t automatically more profitable; the aim is work that suits our equipment, crew, and margins.

That’s what a focused advertising campaign is designed to support. Before launching, we choose the service, project type, and area we want to promote. The ad shows examples of that work, and the enquiry form asks enough about the project to help identify whether it fits before arranging an estimate.

For your company, that could mean more enquiries about complete driveways instead of minor repairs, full landscaping projects instead of routine maintenance, or larger fence installations instead of small fixes. We build the campaign around the work you want to quote, then refine it using your feedback on the enquiries.

The 24-month averages are an illustrative comparison, not a measured monthly trend. Records cover 2025 and 2026 through November 26. Quote value is not booked revenue, and these totals are not attributed entirely to advertising.
